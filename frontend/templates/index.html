Fire safety involving rocket engines and chemical propellants is strictly beyond the scope of NASA's Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI) database. ISS Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR) & Modular ACME Hardware

Located in the US Destiny Laboratory, the CIR is the premier microgravity combustion research facility in orbit. It features an interchangeable Chamber Insert Assembly (CIA) allowing researchers to switch between droplet combustion (FLEX), solid fuels (SOFIE), and modular gaseous burners (ACME).

Combustion Chamber Assembly

110-liter stainless steel hermetic vessel rated up to 100 atmospheres. Features 8 optical quartz/sapphire viewports for cameras, lasers, and radiometers.

Status: Certified for Hot & Cool Flames
Modular ACME Insert Assembly

All 6 experiments in ACME (BRE, CFI-2, CLD Flame, E-FIELD, Flame Design, PEAC) utilized this identical modular hardware in CIR. Burners are swapped via quick-connects without replacing manifolds.

Key NASA Callout: 6-in-1 Hardware Modularity
Burning Rate Emulator (BRE)

Burned gaseous fuels (CH4, C2H4) through flat sintered bronze burners while controlling heat extraction to accurately emulate condensed-phase solid/liquid flammability limits in still atmospheres.

Role: Isolating Gas Flammability of Solids
Microgravity Science Glovebox (MSG)

255-liter sealed working volume operated at negative pressure relative to the cabin (-125 Pa). Hosted the BASS & BASS-II solid burn tests and the SAME smoke aerosol characterizations.

Heritage: BASS / BASS-II & SAME-R Host
Multi-Wavelength Diagnostics Bench

Equipped with 1000 fps high-speed color camera, intensified UV/OH* chemiluminescence imaging, dual broadband radiometers, and Thin-Filament Pyrometry (TFP) for instantaneous 2D flame temperature mapping.

Capabilities: Detects Invisible Cool Flames
Fuel & Oxidizer Gas Delivery (FOGS)

High-precision mass-flow controllers delivering custom blends of O2, N2, CO2, He, CH4, and C2H4 with millisecond-accuracy emergency solenoid isolation valves.

Safety: Triple-Seal Containment
Next-Gen Fire Safety Controls & Earth Spinoffs

Fundamental combustion insights gathered in orbit directly advance life-safety systems both for deep space Artemis exploration habitats and for terrestrial critical infrastructure on Earth.

Fine-Water-Mist Extinguishers

Space Origin (FLEX / CFI): Validated on the ISS to safely extinguish microgravity liquid and solid flames without discharging toxic halon gas or flooding delicate electronics.

Terrestrial Spinoff: Replaces chemical clean-agent and deluge systems in hyperscale server rooms, submarine propulsion compartments, commercial aircraft cabins, and cultural museums. Micro-droplets (10-30 μm) absorb 2260 kJ/kg latent heat, quenching both hot and invisible cool flames while avoiding puddling or electrical shorts.

SAME / DAFT Smoke vs Dust Discriminators

Space Origin (SAME ➔ DAFT Flight Cross-Link): Optical smoke sensors developed for SAME were repurposed for DAFT/DAFT-2 to detect airborne abrasive lunar and Martian regolith dust and prevent false fire alarms in planetary airlocks.

Terrestrial Spinoff: Revolutionized industrial fire safety in harsh mining shafts, wood mills, grain silos, and semiconductor cleanrooms where airborne mineral dust previously caused costly false fire alarms and emergency evacuations.

Advanced Thermal Barriers & Aerogels

Space Origin (BASS & Saffire): Developed for high-temperature containment baffles in the CIR and thermal insulation layers inside Saffire Cygnus enclosure walls.

Terrestrial Spinoff: Ultra-lightweight aerogels and intumescent fabrics now protect urban firefighters with cooler, lighter turnout gear, shield electric vehicle (EV) lithium-ion battery modules against catastrophic thermal runaway propagation, and insulate cryogenic liquid hydrogen tanks.
